## What actually worked

| Change | Evidence | Implication for H200 |
|---|---|---|
| FP16 GEMM policies, compact57, final CLS/split QKV | Integrated beam 2^25 depth8: 124.367 -> 111.024 -> 84.2199 -> 80.2952s across recorded stages | Already substantially represented in the H200 incumbent; do not count again as new work |
| FF1 epilogue 64x32 auto -> 64x64, unchanged MMA/FF2 | Seven alternating full-FFN pairs: 173.685 -> 150.386us, 616.665 -> 712.207 useful TF/s, two batches | Screen FF1 epilogue independently of GEMM tile, with stage/resource evidence |
| FF1 source-C removal at beta=0 | Same-binary full two-slot fixture oracle; seven pairs 149.885 ->145.559us, 714.586 ->735.823 TF/s | Hopper helper already has `ElementC=void`; this exact improvement is exhausted there |
| LN produces native quantized input directly | Synthetic full-four-attention forward ~332k ->455k states/s; not production/solve evidence | Fuse conversion with the producer rather than adding standalone quantization passes |
| Deferred bias + LN1/native QKV producer | Trained full graph 394961 ->407885 states/s; shallow 10/10 solve and replay gate | Preserve residual rounding contract; transfer design, not SM120 instructions |

The 735.823 TF/s result is **quantized FFN only**, not full Transformer,
not FP16, and not H200. A newer void-C/64x128 screen around 762 TF/s remains
preliminary in the inspected snapshot. Do not report it as accepted.

## Tested failures that should not be repeated blindly

- Manual four-CTA DSM / serial shared-memory handoff: about87–131 TF/s versus
  the stock-family ~612–660 TF/s. Removing the global-addressed hidden buffer
  did not compensate for the schedule and live-state costs.
- N256 widening: ~94 TF/s versus ~627; compiler spill-store reports grew
  16–17x. Smaller FF1 N64 also lost ~9.6%, despite lower static spill counts.
  Neither result is proof of a universal hardware limit or dynamic spill cost.
- More slots/larger batch, persisting-L2 hint, repeated attention tile sweeps:
  no consistent retained win in those measured configurations.
- FP16 attention-output -> runtime NVFP4 packing: 219us versus58us FP16.
  Low-bit GEMM-only speed is not the end-to-end result.
- FF2 residual epilogue that rounds residual+bias before LN changed semantics.
  Existing LN statistics use the unrounded float sum while storing half residual.
- A no-output ~921TF/s claim had no MMA in the audited target symbol;
  padded/coded sparse >1PF rates were not useful original-model FLOPs.

The tested register-pack optimization was boundary-specific: adjacent-column
QKV packing helped; applying it to the residual-writing FFN boundary regressed.
Do not replace every shared-memory path with register shuffles by analogy.

## Concrete next H200 decision

1. Keep the verified FP16 baseline and default-off dual-LN experiment intact.
2. Inspect/screen **FF1 epilogue geometry only**, preserving current SM90
   128x128x64 MMA tile, packed weights and QKV/FF2 controls. Inspect actual
   auto-selected epilogue, shared carveout/stages and register/spill reports
   before compiling at most two alternatives. No promised 15.5% H200 gain.
3. Separately pursue **bias/residual + LN + FP8 E4M3 producer** feeding a fast
   Hopper GEMM if conversion cost is recoverable. Keep FP16 rounding where the
   original computation requires it, and gate ranking/solve quality separately
   from quantized-operand correctness. Do not copy Blackwell NVFP4/MXFP8 MMA.
4. Compare complete Stream1 at matched micro/concurrency with counterbalanced
   repeats, then a matched full-pipeline beam/depth. No summing overlapping
   stage timings or treating global-addressed traffic as measured HBM traffic.

NVIDIA architecture reference: Hopper has FP8/FP16 Transformer Engine;
Blackwell adds native MXFP8/NVFP4 formats. See
https://docs.nvidia.com/deeplearning/transformer-engine/user-guide/examples/fp8_primer.html
and https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/technologies/hopper-architecture/.
